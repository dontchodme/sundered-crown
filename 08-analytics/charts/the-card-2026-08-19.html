The card is the cliff, and it travels with the card

Departures per second, per surviving viewer. The two groups put the same 4-second fight card in different places — card-first videos at 0:00–0:04, cold opens at roughly 0:02–0:06 — which makes this a natural experiment. If the card is what costs the audience, the spike has to move with it. It does: the peak sits at second 2 when the card leads, and moves to second 4 when the card is raised ~1.9s in. The cleanest single comparison is second 5 — the card-first video is playing the fight there, the cold open is showing the card, and the cold open loses viewers 1.78× as fast.

While the card is up, step() returns early — the simulation is frozen, ~58% of the frame is card, and the rest sits behind an 80% scrim. It is a four-second dead stop, not a slow passage. The cold open did not remove that cost; it moved it to a moment when a more committed cohort absorbs it. Card-first videos lose 71–75% of the audience present when the card appears. Cold opens lose 55–63%.
